### 3. Financial Performance
- **Annual trend:** Revenue and profit peaked around FY22 and have been choppy since. FY23 net profit Tk783 crore (EPS Tk25.84) on revenue Tk6,640 crore; FY24 net profit recovered to Tk1,356.5 crore (EPS Tk44.78); FY25 net profit fell back to **Tk1,036.62 crore** (EPS Tk34.22) on revenue Tk7,082 crore. Management attributed the FY25 decline to lower revenue, higher finance costs, and increased distribution expenses; all product segments saw sales drops, with the steepest being a ~Tk350 crore fall in air conditioners.
- **Quarterly trend (recovery, then a VAT hit):** Q1 FY26 (Jul–Sep 2025) revenue rose ~19.46% YoY to ~Tk1,451 crore with net profit up 48% to Tk220.89 crore (EPS Tk6.63), driven by higher sales and a 66% drop in finance costs to Tk41.47 crore (from Tk122.89 crore), per The Business Standard. However, Q3 FY26 (Jan–Mar 2026) net profit fell 29% to Tk279.60 crore (EPS Tk8.39 vs Tk11.76 a year earlier) — The Business Standard noted "the VAT rate doubled from 7.5% to 15%… Walton was unable to pass on the additional tax burden to customers." 9M FY26 EPS was Tk19.29 vs Tk20.90, so the full-year FY26 picture is mixed, though operating cash generation strengthened markedly (Q1 NOCFPS Tk14.79 vs Tk3.92).
- **FX impact:** Walton imports components and exports finished goods, so it is sensitive to the taka. In FY23 it booked large FX losses (e.g., ~Tk336 crore in H1) when the taka depreciated rapidly; these have since shrunk (Tk43 crore in a comparable later period), and easing finance/FX costs has been a key driver of the FY26 profit rebound.
- **VAT headwind:** The VAT rate on refrigerators and air conditioners was raised from 7.5% to 15%, pressuring margins/pricing in its two core categories and dragging Q3 FY26 profit.

### 4. Shareholding Pattern
- **Latest verified breakdown (31 August 2025):** Sponsors/directors **61.09%**, institutions **0.70%**, foreigners **0.06%**, general public **38.15%** — per The Business Standard citing DSE data: "the sponsors and directors jointly hold 61.09% of the company's shares, institutional investors hold 0.70%, foreign investors hold 0.06%, and general investors hold 38.15%."
- **Trajectory:** At IPO (2020) Walton floated under 1% of shares. Sponsors held ~99% in early 2022 and ~98.51% as of September 2024. To meet BSEC's 10% minimum free-float directive, sponsor-directors gifted large blocks (~90 million shares over six months) to non-director family members in 2024–25; this mechanically pushed the sponsor category down to ~74% (Oct 2024) and then to ~61% (mid-2025), lifting reported free float above 10% and securing a 2.5-percentage-point corporate tax reduction (22.5% vs 25%).
- **The "thin float" caveat:** Analysts have flagged that much of the ~38% "public" stake is held by founder family members who have retained the gifted shares, so the genuinely tradable float — and thus daily liquidity — remains very small. This is the single most important structural feature of the stock. (A leading stockbroker, quoted anonymously in The Financial Express, warned that "there is a possibility of share price manipulation in the future as a majority of the shares remained within the family.")
- **2026 corporate action:** The Walton Digi-Tech merger involves issuing only a small number of new WALTONHIL shares (a very high exchange ratio against Digi-Tech's shares), so it will not materially change the percentage breakdown or cause meaningful dilution.

## Synthesis (Educational)

**(a) Technical read.** The chart is a long structural downtrend off the 2021 bubble peak, but it has flattened into a range in the high-Tk300s to low-Tk400s over the past year, with the May 2025 low of Tk339 acting as support and the Tk480–528 zone as resistance. Momentum readings are mixed and unreliable here because thin float means low, erratic volume — technical signals on WALTONHIL carry less weight than on a liquid stock. The most honest technical description is "range-bound and basing," not a confirmed reversal.

**(b) Fundamental/valuation read.** On headline multiples the stock screens cheap: ~11.5x trailing earnings, ~1.0x book, and close to its revalued NAV. For a company with a dominant domestic franchise (historically >70% refrigerator market share), low leverage (~22% D/E), mid-teens margins, and strong operating cash flow, those multiples are undemanding relative to history and the broader consumer-durables peer set. The catch: the ~4.2–4.6% cash dividend yield is below the 10% policy rate and well below bank deposit/T-bill yields, so on a pure income basis the stock is not compelling versus cash today — the thesis has to rest on earnings recovery and franchise value, not yield. The FY26 quarterly path (a strong Q1 rebound on lower finance costs, then a Q3 VAT-driven setback) is the key fundamental swing factor; if margins recover as FX stays stable, the trailing P/E understates normalized earnings power.

**(c) Smart-money / shareholding signal.** This is nuanced. Sponsors did not sell into the market out of conviction-driven profit-taking — they gifted shares to family to satisfy a regulator, and the family has largely held. So the drop from 99% to 61% sponsor ownership is a compliance artifact, not an insider exit signal, and the genuine float remains thin. Institutional (0.70%) and foreign (0.06%) ownership is negligible, meaning there is essentially no professional-investor validation of the price. The signal is therefore "founder-controlled, externally under-owned" — read it bullishly (aligned long-term owners) or bearishly (weak price discovery and non-trivial manipulation risk), but recognize there is no classic smart-money endorsement.

**(d) Key risks.** (1) **Liquidity/float** — the thinnest true float of any DSE large-cap; exiting a position can move the price and the quoted price may not reflect a tradable price. (2) **High rates** — a 10% policy rate both raises Walton's finance costs and makes its ~4% yield uncompetitive with cash. (3) **FX** — renewed taka depreciation would inflate imported-component costs and could revive FX losses. (4) **Demand softness** — ~9.4% inflation plus the VAT hike on fridges/ACs pressure unit volumes and margins in core categories. (5) **Governance** — minority independent-director board, founder concentration, and a related-party merger that minority holders have limited power to influence.

**(e) Balanced summary.** WALTONHIL is a high-quality operating business trading at a low valuation for identifiable reasons: a thin, founder-dominated float; a cyclical earnings dip now partially recovering; and a dividend yield that loses out to a 10% policy rate. It is best understood as a franchise-value/recovery story rather than an income or momentum play. The recent profit rebound, FX stabilization, low leverage, and the Digi-Tech consolidation are genuine positives; the structural liquidity and governance issues and the macro squeeze on consumer demand are genuine negatives. Where one lands depends largely on how much weight is placed on the cheap multiple and franchise versus the float/liquidity overhang and the opportunity cost of a sub-policy-rate yield.
